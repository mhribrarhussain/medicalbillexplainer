How to Dispute an Unfair Hospital Bill

A careful review can help you identify duplicate charges, missing insurance adjustments, and amounts that need clarification. Follow this five-step process and use the letter templates to communicate with billing offices.

The 5-Step Hospital Bill Dispute Protocol

Step 1: Request an Immediate 60-Day Billing Hold

The moment you suspect an error or unaffordable charge, call hospital billing and say:

"I am formally disputing this invoice and request an administrative 60-day billing hold while the charges are reviewed. Please confirm this account will not be referred to collections."

A provider may have its own hold policy, and a request does not guarantee that collection or reporting activity will stop. Ask for the decision and any deadline in writing.

Step 2: Request an Itemized Statement

A summary statement may use broad descriptions such as "Specialty Pharmacy: $2,800" or "Lab: $950". Ask the provider what itemized statement and coding details it can provide for the account.

Use Letter Template #1 above to formally request an itemized statement with all 5-digit CPT procedure codes and revenue codes.

Step 3: Cross-Check Against Your Insurance EOB

Compare every line on the itemized bill with your insurance Explanation of Benefits (EOB):

  • If the hospital is in-network, compare the provider balance with the EOB's patient-responsibility figure and confirm any difference with both parties.
  • A difference between the Billed Amount and Allowed Amount may be a contractual adjustment, but the outcome depends on network status, plan rules, claim details, and applicable law.

Step 4: Audit for the "Big 4" Hospital Billing Errors

1. Duplicate Charges

Check if the same CPT code appears twice on the same day (e.g. being billed twice for a blood draw, chest X-ray, or IV dose).

2. Upcoding

A simple 15-minute visit coded as a high-complexity Level 5 visit (CPT 99285 or 99215) without medical justification.

3. Unbundling

Billing tests or surgical supplies separately when AMA coding rules mandate they be included in a single package fee.

4. Phantom / Cancelled Services

Charges for medications, equipment, or nursing trays that were ordered by a physician but never actually administered.

Step 5: Apply for Non-Profit Charity Care (IRS 501(r))

Tax-exempt hospitals generally must maintain a Financial Assistance Policy under IRS Section 501(r). Ask the hospital for its policy, eligibility rules, application deadline, and required documents; discounts vary by facility and household circumstances.

Even if you have insurance, you may still ask about financial assistance. Eligibility and any discount depend on the hospital's policy, your household circumstances, and the documentation required. Use Letter Template #3 to begin that request.

Federal No Surprises Act (2022): Your Shield

If you received emergency care, or if you went to an in-network hospital but were treated by an out-of-network doctor (such as an anesthesiologist, radiologist, or ER doctor you didn't choose), federal law strictly prohibits the provider from balance billing you.

Key Protections Under the Law:
  • You only owe your standard in-network cost-sharing (copay/deductible).
  • The out-of-network provider cannot send you a bill for the remaining balance.
  • If a provider violates this law, you can file a complaint with CMS at 1-800-985-3059.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I negotiate a discount if I pay cash immediately?

Yes. Almost all hospitals and clinics offer a "Prompt-Pay Cash Discount" of 20% to 40% if you agree to pay the settled balance in a lump sum over the phone. Call billing and ask: "What cash settlement discount can you offer if I pay off this account in full today?"

Can a medical bill affect my credit report?

Credit-reporting treatment can depend on the debt, reporting agency policies, payment status, amount, and current law. Do not assume that sending a dispute automatically pauses reporting. Keep copies of your dispute, check the provider's response, and review current guidance from the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau and the credit-reporting agencies.

What should I do if a hospital ignores my dispute letter?

If the hospital fails to respond within 30 days and continues sending collection notices, file an official grievance with your State Department of Insurance, your state Attorney General's consumer protection bureau, and the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB).
